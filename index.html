Research Overview

What if time is not something the universe fundamentally possesses, but something its physical processes produce?

The Unified Theory of Timelessness (UTT) is a theoretical physics framework developed by independent researcher Keith Root to investigate the emergence and physical nature of time. UTT addresses the Problem of Time by asking whether observable temporal order can emerge from established physical processes rather than requiring time to exist as a fundamental background against which the universe evolves.

The research develops this question from within the established structures of General Relativity, quantum mechanics, and thermodynamics. General Relativity provides an extraordinarily successful description of spacetime, proper time, gravitational redshift, and gravitational time dilation. In the conventional explanatory hierarchy, proper time occupies the primitive invariant role through which physical evolution is parameterized.

UTT investigates whether phase-based physical realization can precede the conventional assignment of proper time as the primitive invariant quantity governing physical evolution. Under this interpretation, gravitational conditions differentiate the spectral realization of physical systems, from which temporal differentiation first emerges; unitary quantum evolution preserves that realized structure; and interaction with increasingly complex many-body systems incorporates its consequences into persistent physical history. Thermodynamic irreversibility then establishes a growing distinction between recoverable local dynamics and an inherited past, connecting the emergence of temporal history with the physical Arrow of Time.

General Relativity remains essential within this architecture. Rather than being displaced, relativistic geometry provides the invariant relational structure through which separately accumulated physical histories become comparable across curved spacetime. Measurements involving atomic clocks, optical clocks, frequency metrology, interferometry, gravitational redshift, and relativistic time dilation therefore provide an operational connection between quantum phase and frequency and the proper-time relationships described by relativity.

UTT consequently approaches emergent time as a compound physical organization: physical possibilities become realized, realized structure persists, its consequences become incorporated into history, and separately accumulated histories become relationally comparable.

Time is not removed from physics. It is assembled by it.
