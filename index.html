The Emergence of Time from within General Relativity's Invariant Formalism

Unified Theory of Timelessness (UTT)
Keith Root
Independent Researcher
ORCID: 0009-0002-3544-4149
Read the full paper on Zenodo

What if…

the path to emergent time is already accessible from within the very same physics that taught us to regard time as fundamental?

General Relativity provides our most successful description of temporal behavior. Within its invariant spacetime formalism, proper time accumulates along a worldline and serves as the geometric measure against which physical evolution is conventionally parameterized. From this formalism comes one of modern physics' deepest assumptions: physical systems evolve with respect to time.

UTT does not abandon that relationship. Physical systems continue to evolve in time, and General Relativity retains its invariant geometry, proper-time relations, and empirical predictions. What changes is not the operational role of General Relativity and proper time, but their position within UTT’s explanatory hierarchy. Within this framework, time still very much exists exactly as we experience and measure it, yet it no longer needs to be fundamental.

And here the implications become more intriguing. Time can emerge from physical processes and then become the temporal structure through which their subsequent evolution is organized and parameterized. The compound processes from which time emerges ultimately come to evolve within the very temporal order their own organization has made possible.

Wait. If time wasn't there at the beginning, how did anything evolve sufficiently to produce it?

Unified Theory of Timelessness (UTT) explores a permissive pathway within General Relativity’s invariant formalism—one that admits an alternative ontological reordering of physical primitives through which temporal order can lawfully emerge from physical differentiation and organization rather than stand as their prerequisite.

UTT demonstrates that time can be emergent in origin while remaining fully operative in the physical evolution that follows from its emergence. Time therefore need not be fundamental in order to become physically real, measurable, and indispensable to the subsequent organization of physical evolution.

UTT is not a theory of a universe without time. It is a theory of how a timeless physical foundation can become a universe with time. The apparent opposition between emergent time and evolution in time therefore dissolves: emergence describes how temporal order comes to exist, while General Relativity describes how physical systems behave once that temporal order is established.

A central finding of UTT is that the emergence of time and subsequent evolution in time are not competing descriptions of physical reality, but successive organizational regimes in a compound architecture of temporal behavior. Time can be emergent in origin and subsequently become fully operative as the temporal structure through which further physical evolution is organized and parameterized. The physical processes from which temporal order emerges therefore need not remain timeless: once temporal organization is established, those same physical processes become incorporated within—and subsequently evolve through—the temporal architecture their organization has made possible.

Emergence does not require the emergent property to remain external to the processes from which it arose. Once established, the emergent organization can become a governing condition of their subsequent behavior.

That principle reaches beyond time. It reveals a recursive organizational structure at the heart of UTT: physical organization can generate conditions that did not govern its own origin, yet become indispensable to everything that follows. A more primitive lower-order process gives rise to a higher-order organization; that higher-order organization then incorporates the processes from which it emerged and becomes part of the conditions governing their subsequent realization. The consequence is profound: the organizational conditions governing a physical system after emergence need not have governed the conditions from which that organization first arose. Emergence need not change the underlying physics; it can change the organizational regime within which that physics subsequently operates.

The apparent paradox—How can anything evolve before time exists?—therefore rests upon an assumption UTT does not require: that the physical processes sufficient to produce temporal organization must themselves already occur in time. UTT instead distinguishes the lawful physical differentiation from which temporal order can emerge from the subsequent evolution that becomes parameterized by that temporal order. These are not two competing accounts of the same process. They occupy different positions within the same organizational hierarchy.

Time therefore need not precede physical organization in order eventually to govern its evolution. It can arise as a consequence of physical organization and, once established, become one of the conditions through which subsequent physical organization proceeds. The universe need not begin in time for time ultimately to become indispensable to the universe.

A Permissive Path Within General Relativity

Retaining General Relativity in full is therefore not merely a defensive feature of UTT; it is structurally necessary to its compound architecture of time. General Relativity already embodies a reciprocal relationship between physical structure and time: spacetime geometry determines the accumulation of proper time, while proper time in turn provides the temporal measure through which physical evolution is described. Its mathematical formalism and its conventional ontological treatment of time are therefore deeply intertwined.

That relationship is precisely why the placement of General Relativity within UTT matters. UTT does not remove GR from the architecture of time; it places GR at the exact point in the explanatory hierarchy where its formalism becomes fully operative. The timeless principles from which UTT begins first establish the prerequisites of temporal organization; once that organization emerges, General Relativity supplies the invariant geometric structure through which physical evolution is temporally parameterized and locally accumulated histories become relationally comparable.

GR therefore appears in UTT not as an accommodation made necessary by established physics, but as an expected consequence of the hierarchy itself. Its familiar relationship with proper time emerges precisely where that relationship is required.

The distinction between these regimes is consequential. The emergent principles proposed by UTT are no more intended to replace General Relativity within the mature temporal domain than the temporal formalism of General Relativity is suited to describe the formally timeless conditions from which UTT proposes that temporal order emerges.

Each becomes physically explanatory at its appropriate level of organization. Their relationship is therefore not one of competing descriptions applied to the same domain, but of successive explanatory regimes: UTT addresses the organizational conditions through which temporal order can arise; General Relativity describes the invariant geometric relationships through which physical evolution proceeds once that temporal order has become established.

Proper time consequently remains indispensable to physical evolution, but its role changes. It need not stand primordial at the ontological beginning of the account in order to become fully operative within the temporal order that emerges.

The permissive gateway appears within General Relativity itself. GR occupies the proper hierarchical position in UTT because the very structure of its formalism presupposes the mature reciprocal relationship between geometry, proper time, and physical evolution that UTT is trying to explain the emergence of.

UTT’s emergent formalism describes how a lawful reordering of established physical processes allows the prerequisites of temporal organization to arise, after which those same processes become organized by the temporal structure that emerges from them. There is no contradiction in General Relativity remaining indispensable to that architecture. On the contrary, its indispensability is precisely what the hierarchy predicts. UTT provides a lawful and coherent pathway to an emergent time long regarded as conceptually possible, yet notoriously difficult to reconcile with the temporal foundations of established physics. Remarkably, that difficulty begins to recede through a comparatively intelligible reordering of physical relationships already present within it.

General Relativity therefore remains indispensable to the resulting compound architecture of time, even though proper time no longer needs to bear the ontological burden of being fundamental. UTT does not diminish GR by placing it beyond the origin of time; it gives GR a precise and necessary position within the temporal architecture that emerges.

Seen in this light, UTT’s proposal is considerably less radical than its conclusion may initially suggest. Once the invariant physical relationships are preserved and time is considered as a compound architecture assembled across successive levels of physical organization, the possibility of emergent time begins to occupy a natural position within the hierarchy. Temporal emergence is placed upstream of the established temporal physics it must ultimately produce, while the familiar comparative regime of proper time and General Relativity remains fully operative downstream. Nothing that established physics successfully describes is displaced; what changes is the ontological ordering through which that successful physics is reached.

This makes the alternative surprisingly economical. Emergent time need not compete with the temporal world already described by physics, because its explanatory task lies at a different level: to account for how the conditions required by that temporal world can arise. General Relativity then remains exactly where its extraordinary empirical success requires it to remain—within the mature temporal regime—while no longer being required to carry the additional ontological burden of making proper time primordial.

The stronger burden therefore falls upon any claim that the familiar temporal ordering represents the only physically admissible architecture. If the same invariant relationships can participate coherently within another explanatory hierarchy while preserving their empirical consequences, then a single, mutually exclusive ontological ordering becomes increasingly difficult to regard as uniquely required by the physics itself.

UTT therefore does not need to displace the conventional architecture in order to matter. The existence of a second lawful and internally coherent pathway would itself be consequential, because it would demonstrate that invariant physical relationships can constrain what nature does without necessarily prescribing a unique ontological interpretation of how those relationships must be organized or understood. Multiple ontological interpretations may therefore remain permissible so long as each preserves the invariant structure, empirical relationships, and observable consequences demanded by the underlying physics.

UTT stands on its own formalism within that permissible space. Its significance lies in showing that emergent time and mutually comparable relativistic time need not constitute competing or mutually exclusive accounts. They can occupy successive and complementary levels within the same compound architecture: temporal order emerges from a more primitive organization of established physical processes, and, once established, becomes the framework within which those same processes evolve and their locally accumulated histories become mutually comparable.

In this way, a longstanding conceptual barrier begins to dissolve without requiring substantial disruption to accepted physics. The explanatory burden shifts from assuming time as fundamental to accounting for its emergence, while the invariant relationships, empirical predictions, and mature temporal structure of General Relativity remain intact. The result is almost unexpectedly economical: what initially appears to require a radical departure from established physics instead emerges through a comparatively elementary reordering of relationships already present within it. The pathway appears unfamiliar not because the necessary physics was absent, but because its familiar ontological ordering had obscured another permissible interpretation.

UTT is therefore best understood not as a competing replacement for the conventional temporal interpretation of General Relativity, but as an alternative explanatory hierarchy constructed under the same invariant physical constraints. The conventional interpretation remains fully applicable within the mature temporal regime; UTT asks whether those same invariant relationships also permit a lawful account of how that regime can emerge.

The distinction is fundamental: invariant physics constrains the admissible structure of physical reality, but it need not uniquely determine the ontological ordering through which that structure is explained. UTT occupies precisely that opening.

The relevant starting point is the gravitational proper-time relation, because it expresses how spacetime geometry determines the rate at which proper time accumulates relative to a distant coordinate reference. It therefore sits directly at the conventional interface between gravitational structure and temporal evolution—the relationship whose explanatory ordering UTT seeks to reconsider. For a stationary observer in the Schwarzschild exterior, that relationship is expressed through the familiar square-root scaling:

\[\frac{d\tau}{dt}=\sqrt{g_{00}}=\sqrt{1-\frac{2GM}{Rc^2}}\]

Here, \(d\tau/dt\) gives the rate of local proper-time accumulation relative to coordinate time, while \(g_{00}\) carries the gravitational dependence through the Schwarzschild metric. As gravitational potential changes, the relation determines the corresponding gravitational scaling of proper time.

The relevance of the square-root relationship for UTT is that this same invariant relationship can be written in an algebraically equivalent exponential form. The observable gravitational scaling remains unchanged, but its mathematical representation no longer needs to remain confined to the square-root form through which proper-time accumulation is conventionally expressed.

Defining the dimensionless gravitational parameter:

\[\alpha_g=\frac{1}{2}\,\,\ln(g_{00})=\frac{1}{2}\,\,\ln\left(1-\frac{2GM}{Rc^2}\right)\]

gives the exactly equivalent exponential relation:

\[e^{\alpha_g}=\sqrt{g_{00}}=\sqrt{1-\frac{2GM}{Rc^2}}=\frac{d\tau}{dt}\]

Nothing about the observable physics has changed. The square-root and exponential forms encode the same invariant gravitational scaling, and the empirical predictions remain intact. What changes is the mathematical representation through which that scaling can participate in the explanatory architecture. The square-root form expresses gravitational scaling through geometric proper-time accumulation; the equivalent exponential form preserves that same invariant relationship while allowing it to enter a mathematical architecture compatible with the exponential structure of quantum phase evolution.

The Exponential Gateway

That change of representation exposes the permissive gateway. Written exponentially, gravitational scaling enters a mathematical architecture naturally compatible with the exponential phase structure already governing quantum evolution. The unitary time-evolution operator of quantum mechanics is expressed as:

\[\Psi(t)=e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\,\,\Psi_0\]

The significance of the exponential formalism within UTT is fundamental.

UTT does not claim discovery of a new gravitational scaling law. The logarithmic lift is mathematically elementary; what UTT contributes is the recognition of how much established physics can be coherently reorganized once that representation is allowed to participate in the explanatory hierarchy. Without the logarithmic lift, the alternative ontological ordering would remain tethered to the square-root representation of proper-time accumulation and would ultimately collapse back into the geometric paradigm from which it was intended to depart.

The exponential representation provides the formal separation UTT requires. It preserves the invariant content of General Relativity while releasing gravitational scaling from an exclusively geometric representation. In doing so, it brings gravitational scaling and quantum phase evolution—two physical regimes traditionally treated separately—into a shared exponential architecture without conflating their distinct physical meanings.

This shared architecture provides UTT with a potentially deeper perspective on the quantum-gravity problem. Within UTT, gravitational potential provides the asymmetry required to differentiate otherwise symmetric timeless principles into locally realized physical states, while quantum phase evolution provides the continuous dynamical structure through which those realizations can be preserved and propagated. General Relativity and quantum mechanics thereby enter the same explanatory progression precisely because each supplies a distinct organizational function within a common physical architecture.

From this perspective, the apparent divide between quantum mechanics and gravitation may reflect something deeper than the difficulty of mathematically unifying two successful theories. It may also reflect their conventional placement on separate explanatory planes. UTT’s exponential formalism instead places them within a continuous hierarchy: gravitational asymmetry constrains physical realization, quantum phase preserves its continuity, thermodynamic incorporation converts realized consequences into irreversible history, and General Relativity ultimately provides the invariant geometry through which those accumulated histories become relationally comparable.

Quantum gravity therefore acquires a different point of entry. The question is no longer only how gravity might be quantized or how spacetime might emerge from quantum structure, but whether quantum and gravitational behavior are already complementary expressions of a deeper organizational continuity whose successive regimes ultimately produce the compound architecture of time.

From this perspective, the divide between quantum mechanics and gravitation may admit a different pathway toward mathematical unification. Their relationship need not be understood through mutual exclusivity, nor require one successful description of nature to be reduced to the other. UTT instead suggests a recursive hierarchy in which more primitive physical processes are progressively incorporated into higher-order forms of organization while remaining continuously operative within them. Quantum and gravitational behavior can therefore retain their distinct physical meanings while participating within the same developing architecture.

This reiterates a broader principle emerging throughout UTT: higher-order organization does not erase the physical processes from which it arises; it inherits them. What was independently operative at one level becomes incorporated into the organizational conditions of the next. The apparent boundaries between physical regimes may therefore represent not failures of unification, but the interfaces through which physical organization becomes progressively richer.

This is where UTT's distinction lies. The novelty is not the algebraic identity: \(e^{\alpha_g}=\sqrt{g_{00}}\,\) itself, but the recognition of what that equivalent representation permits when placed within a much broader hierarchy of established physical principles. UTT’s significance therefore lies in what becomes visible when the exponential form of gravitational scaling, quantum phase evolution, unitary continuity, thermodynamic irreversibility, and relativistic comparison are organized through that representation into a remarkably coherent, continuous explanatory progression—one through which temporal order can emerge without altering the invariant physical relationships from which the progression began.

The exponential formalism therefore gives UTT an autonomous mathematical language through which this alternative ordering can be carried forward without collapsing back into the geometric interpretation of proper time. The invariant gravitational relation remains unchanged, yet its representation now permits gravitational constraint and quantum phase structure to participate within a common mathematical architecture.

UTT therefore asks a deeper question: Can phase-based physical realization coherently precede geometric proper-time accumulation, allowing time itself to emerge while preserving the invariant structure of General Relativity?

The Architecture of Emergent Time

UTT consequently does not treat time as arising from a single physical structure, but as a compound architecture assembled through successive levels of physical organization. Each level introduces an organizational capacity not available to the preceding level, while preserving and incorporating the physical consequences already established. Time emerges through this progression rather than appearing fully formed at any single boundary.

Physical Possibility → Phase-based Realization → Unitary Continuity →
Thermodynamic Incorporation → Irreversible History → Relativistic Comparison

Physical possibility establishes the invariant domain of what may be realized. Phase-based realization introduces differentiation within that domain, converting admissible possibility into locally realized physical structure. Unitary quantum evolution preserves the continuity of that realization without yet producing irreversible history. Thermodynamic many-body incorporation then distributes the consequences of realization into progressively wider physical dependencies, transforming preserved realization into inherited, irreversible history. General Relativity completes the mature temporal architecture by supplying the invariant relational geometry through which locally accumulated histories become mutually comparable across curved spacetime.

The progression is therefore cumulative rather than mutually exclusive. Each organizational regime inherits what the preceding regime makes possible and carries its consequences forward into a richer level of physical organization. What begins as timeless physical possibility ultimately becomes differentiated realization, preserved continuity, inherited history, and finally a relativistically comparable temporal world.

Remarkably Little New Machinery

And here lies perhaps the most surprising feature of the proposal: with the exception of its Jacobian-conjugate spectral assignments, UTT requires remarkably little new physical machinery. The invariant interval, gravitational scaling, action, quantum phase evolution, unitary dynamics, thermodynamic irreversibility, and relativistic comparison are already familiar structures of modern physics. What changes is their ontological ordering—and with it, the explanatory architecture that becomes visible when these established principles are allowed to participate within a single continuum of temporal emergence.

No external temporal mechanism needs to be introduced to drive this organizational progression. General Relativity need not be discarded or rewritten. Quantum mechanics and thermodynamics remain intact. Nor is exotic mathematics introduced merely to make emergent time possible. Instead, structures ordinarily treated within separate explanatory domains assume complementary positions within a continuous progression from physical possibility to realization, continuity, irreversible history, and relativistic temporal comparison.

Beyond the Origin of Time

The significance of UTT extends beyond the immediate question of how temporal order emerges. By organizing gravitational scaling, quantum phase evolution, unitary continuity, thermodynamic irreversibility, and relativistic comparison within a continuous explanatory hierarchy, the theory presents a set of new conceptual relationships for scientific contemplation across several foundational problems in physics.

If thermodynamic irreversibility represents the incorporation of realized consequences into progressively distributed physical history, UTT provides another perspective from which to examine foundational problems such as the Past Hypothesis and the origin of temporal asymmetry. Likewise, if gravitational scaling, phase structure, and the conditions of physical realization participate within a deeper organizational relationship, the framework introduces new ways of considering extreme gravitational regimes, including those associated with Hawking radiation.

Perhaps most significantly, taken together with the perspective already developed concerning the quantum-gravity problem, these extensions represent fresh conceptual possibilities with potentially far-reaching consequences made accessible by the theory. Their significance lies in the new questions UTT makes possible: whether the emergence of relativistic temporal structure from formally timeless principles may provide a key to understanding how apparently distinct physical regimes arise as successive organizational expressions of a deeper physical continuity.

One of the most intriguing possibilities concerns what our deepest models of physical reality might reveal once the dimensional structure of spacetime is considered within this hierarchy. General Relativity describes a four-dimensional spacetime in which three spatial dimensions and one temporal dimension participate within a unified geometric structure. UTT does not challenge the validity of that structure within the regime in which it is physically operative. It raises a different question: whether the entire spatial-temporal architecture described by that geometry may itself be supported by a more primitive organization whose governing principles are not fundamentally temporal.

From this perspective, the conceptual shift is subtle but potentially profound. Rather than beginning with three dimensions of spatial extension evolving within an already established temporal order, one may ask whether both spatial differentiation and temporal evolution ultimately depend upon invariant relationships that are themselves timeless. Space and time would remain fully real within the physical architecture that emerges, while neither would necessarily exhaust the character of the foundations from which that architecture becomes possible.

This possibility changes the direction of the foundational question. Instead of asking only how matter and fields evolve through four-dimensional spacetime, UTT invites consideration of what physical organization must already exist for spatial and temporal differentiation to become possible at all. The familiar spacetime manifold would then represent not the abandonment of timeless foundations, but their higher-order realization: a regime in which invariant physical relationships have become organized into the spatial and temporal structure through which subsequent physical evolution can occur.

Such a possibility remains speculative, but it illustrates the broader reach of the framework. If temporal organization can emerge without displacing the invariant physics from which it arises, then it becomes reasonable to ask whether other structures conventionally treated as foundational may likewise represent higher-order expressions of a deeper physical continuity.

What might physics look like if spacetime is not the foundation upon which physical reality is built, but one of the organized structures that physical reality becomes?

From this perspective, quantum behavior, thermodynamic irreversibility, gravitational structure, and relativistic time need not be understood as mutually exclusive explanatory domains. They may instead represent progressively incorporated levels of physical organization whose deeper relationship becomes visible only when their conventional explanatory ordering is reconsidered. UTT therefore invites consideration of whether some of the boundaries that appear to divide foundational physics are not boundaries in nature itself, but interfaces between different levels through which nature becomes organized.

What If the Physics Was Already There?

The deeper implication returns us to General Relativity itself. A formalism conventionally interpreted as beginning with primitive temporal accumulation may already admit another lawful explanatory path—one in which temporal order emerges while the invariant relationships and empirical success of GR remain intact.

If so, the physics required to move beyond time as a primitive may not lie outside General Relativity at all. It may already be present within its invariant structure, not obscured by the mathematics, but by the explanatory ordering traditionally assigned to that mathematics.

What changes, then, is not General Relativity, but where General Relativity is placed within the hierarchy of physical explanation. Proper time remains real, measurable, and indispensable; spacetime geometry retains its invariant relationships; and relativistic predictions remain untouched.

What UTT changes is the requirement that fundamental time must stand at the beginning of physics in order ultimately to govern it. If that path proves physically meaningful, the question confronting us changes. It is no longer simply: What new physics must be invented to explain the origin of time?

It becomes something more intriguing: What if the physics required for emergent time was already there? What if the obstacle was not a missing equation, but an ontological assignment so familiar that its status as an assignment had largely disappeared from view?

The seeds of emergent time may therefore lie neither beyond General Relativity nor in a replacement for it, but within the invariant relationships that made General Relativity successful in the first place—relationships whose empirical consequences have been known for more than a century, while the alternative ontological pathway they permit has remained largely unexplored.

There is a deeper philosophical implication. A physical relationship can remain invariant even when our understanding of what is ontologically fundamental within that relationship changes. Empirical success establishes the extraordinary fidelity of General Relativity's relational structure; it does not necessarily require that every element of the ontology conventionally associated with that structure occupy the same position in the explanatory hierarchy. UTT explores precisely that distinction. The mathematics is preserved, the physical relationships are preserved, and the observable consequences are preserved—while the presumed direction of explanation is allowed to change.

The established physical principles therefore remain mathematically intact. What changes is their explanatory ordering—and with that reordering, a different architecture becomes visible. Time no longer needs to be placed at the beginning of physical evolution. It can emerge from physical organization and subsequently become one of the conditions through which that organization continues to evolve.

Perhaps fundamentality itself is the deeper assumption being tested. What appears fundamental from within an established physical regime need not have been fundamental to the conditions from which that regime emerged. Time can be indispensable without being primordial.

Sometimes an advance in our understanding of the physical world does not require new physics. Sometimes it requires seeing the physics we already possess in a different order.

In this theory, time is not removed from physics. It is assembled by it—and, once assembled, becomes the temporal order through which the very physical processes that gave rise to it continue their evolution. What emerges from physical organization ultimately becomes part of the organization of physics itself.
